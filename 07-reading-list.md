# Reading list

Ordered as a reading path.

1. **Framework — read first.** Poisot et al. 2021, *A roadmap toward predicting species interaction networks (across space and time).* Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 376:20210063. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2021.0063 — the framework this paper extends; their across-space prediction is the bar to clear.

2. **Framing constraint — most important.** Blanchet, Cazelles & Gravel 2020, *Co-occurrence is not evidence of ecological interactions.* Ecol. Lett. 23:1050–1063. https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13525 — why claims must be *opportunity*, not interaction. Pair with Coelho et al. 2024 (Nat. Ecol. Evol., https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-023-02254-y): ~20% of co-occurrences are real interactions.

3. **Methods hygiene.** Dormann et al. 2018, *Biotic interactions in species distribution modelling: 10 questions to guide interpretation and avoid false conclusions.* Glob. Ecol. Biogeogr. 27:1004–1016. https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.12759 — the cautions a reviewer will hold you to; "constant in space and time" is the assumption PPE relaxes.

4. **Phenology-specific framing.** Rafferty et al. 2025, *Complexities of Phenological Shifts for Plant–Pollinator Interactions and Ways Forward.* Integr. Comp. Biol. https://doi.org/10.1093/icb/icaf034 — temporal overlap is an *opportunity* metric, not strength or mismatch. Scope claims accordingly.

5. **Bias vocabulary.** Carlen et al. 2024, *A framework for contextualizing social-ecological biases in contributory science data.* People and Nature. https://doi.org/10.1002/pan3.10592 — participation / detectability / sampling / preference filters. Plant-side bias = preference + detectability; pollinator-side = detectability.

6. **Empirical precedent — for the intro.** Bosenbecker et al. 2023, *Contrasting nation-wide citizen science and expert collected data on hummingbird–plant interactions.* Perspect. Ecol. Conserv. 21:164–171. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pecon.2023.03.004 — citizen vs expert interaction data: <40% shared partners, photo count drives records. Near one-line motivation.

Also relevant: the presence-only evaluation literature (Boyce et al. 2002, the Boyce index) for the metric, and standard SDM background-sampling practice for constructing the eval. For the SINR extension, Cole et al. 2023, *Spatial Implicit Neural Representations for Global-Scale Species Mapping* (ICML 2023, arXiv:2306.02564).
